House Cleaning in Morganton, NC: What a Real Reset Looks Like

The full Reset in Burke County — the same 49-point standard our Hickory customers get, with every room checked twice.

No surface tidy dressed up as a deep clean, and no guessing at your home with a posted chart. You send us your home, we price that home, and you approve the price before the clean.

Morganton Homes Build Up Their Own Way

Morganton is the seat of Burke County — a mountain-gateway city on the Catawba River, with Lake James at its doorstep and neighborhoods that run from historic in-town streets to quiet roads toward the Blue Ridge. It’s beautiful country, and the homes here carry everything that comes with four real seasons: pollen in the spring, dust in the summer, and a deep layer that builds up while life stays busy.

That buildup is the honest reason a Morganton home needs more than a quick pass. Spring pollen settles into vents, windowsills, and ceiling fans. Summer dust works its way behind furniture and under cushions. Kitchens collect cooked-on grease on the stovetop and inside the oven. Bathrooms gather soap scum in grout lines and hard-water marks on chrome. None of it shows up all at once — it layers, week by week, until the home feels tired even when it’s technically tidy. A real clean takes the layers off. That’s what a Reset is for.

What the Reset Covers in Your Home

A Reset is our signature whole-home deep clean, and it runs on a written list — not on whoever shows up deciding what counts as clean that day. The full list is the 49-Point Inspection, and you can read every point of it before you ever book. Here’s the shape of it:

Kitchen: the room that works hardest

Countertops and backsplash scrubbed and disinfected, sink scrubbed and disinfected, stovetop degreased, cabinet fronts wet-cleaned — and the part most cleans skip or sell back as an extra: the oven cleaned inside and out, the refrigerator cleaned inside, the microwave cleaned inside and out. Appliance interiors are standard with us, never an add-on. Floors vacuumed and mopped, windowsills cleaned, trash emptied.

Bathrooms: scrubbed, disinfected, and shined

Tub and shower scrubbed, disinfected, and rinsed. Toilet disinfected inside and out. Sinks, countertops, and mirrors cleaned. Chrome fixtures shined, light fixtures cleaned, floors disinfected, towels neatly hung and folded. The small things — a windowsill, a light fixture — are on the list because they’re the things a rushed clean leaves behind.

Bedrooms and living areas: the whole home, not the show rooms

Beds made with linens changed, furniture dusted top, front, and underneath, floors vacuumed and washed — under the bed where it’s reachable. In the living areas, upholstered furniture gets vacuumed between, under, and behind the cushions. Cobwebs come down from ceilings, vents, and light fixtures. High-touch surfaces — handles, switches, remotes — get disinfected. Baseboards get lightly dusted throughout, honestly stated: a light clean, not a deep scrub. And the whole job gets checked twice before anyone calls it done.

You can read all 49 points in our post on the 49-point house cleaning checklist — it’s the same list our partners work from on every job.

Deep Clean First, Then Keep It There

The question most Morganton homeowners ask us is whether they need a deep clean or a regular one. The honest answer is almost always the same: start with the Reset. A recurring clean maintains a clean home — it doesn’t create one out of a home that’s been building layers for a season or a year. Once the Reset has taken the layers off, a standing recurring clean keeps it there, running the same 49-point inspection every visit so the home never slides back.

If you’re weighing the two, our guide to Deep Clean vs. Regular Clean walks through the signs that tell you which one your home actually needs.

Moving In or Out in Morganton

The Reset has a second form for moves: the Move-In / Move-Out Reset. Moving out, the clean that matters is the one your landlord walks through — inside appliances and cabinets, the spots that cost deposits. Moving in, it’s the clean that lets you settle into a home that is actually yours, not the last family’s dust. Same 49-point standard, same double-check, same signed walkthrough — timed for after the home is empty and before the final walkthrough, so the work lands where it counts.
